# Vāgbhaṭa's Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya, with Aruṇadatta's Sarvāṅgasundarā and Hemādri's Āyurvedarasāyana: word spacing and verse tagging — spots to check

Word spacing was added in 2026-11 by 155 agents, one per piece of the TEI file
(tools/spacing/, see its README). Each agent reported up to five places it was
unsure of; they are listed below as "NNN l.LINE", where NNN is the piece and
LINE the line within that piece. Use the form quoted to find the place in
astangahrdaya-full.xml (search for it; after the avagraha pass te'pi is te 'pi).

Decisions taken across the whole text (as for the Śārṅgadharasaṃhitā, the
Mādhavanidāna and the Carakasaṃhitā; see tools/spacing/INSTRUCTIONS.md):
- the notes are not spaced.
- avagraha: a space before ' after e/o everywhere (te 'pi); ā' (tathā'pi) stays joined.
- particles split everywhere (tathā ca, yad vā, na hi, yady api, ity arthaḥ, yad
  uktaṃ, tad yathā, kiñ ca, kiṃ vā …); joined: tadanu, tadanantaraṃ, kintu,
  parantu, athavā, itastataḥ.
- a doubled consonant at a word junction written once: left joined; typos are left
  uncorrected and usually joined.

Decided by the editor for this text (2026-11), and applied throughout:
- ity ādi and its cases are two words (ity ādinā, ity ādibhiḥ, ity ādiślokaḥ);
  this differs from the three earlier texts, where ityādi is joined.
- ity antena is two words (also differs from the earlier texts).
- commentary lemmata fused with iti follow from this: athety ādi, cety antena,
  gurv ity ādi.
- yat kiñcit, punar uktam, prāg uktam, prāg eva are split. Derived forms stay
  joined: punaruktatvāt, punaruktatā, punaruktir, apunaruktam, yatkiñcana-.
- indravajrāvṛttam is always joined, like the other metre names compounded with
  -vṛttam (śālinīvṛttam, upajātivṛttam). Where the source inflects the name it
  stays two words (upajātir vṛttam, śārdūlavikrīḍitaṃ vṛttam).
- hi yasmād arthe (also paścād arthe, by analogy).
- a typo seen while applying these: the source prints "hetā vityādi" (for hetāv
  ity ādi) in the last chapter; left as it is, since no source space is removed.

Policy questions for the editor (still open):
- kiñ ca (13) and kiṃ vā (4) are written as two words. Is that right, or kiñca / kiṃvā?
- kin tarhi (12) is written as two words, although kintu is joined. Keep?
- iti pad- (4), as in "iti padacchedaḥ", is split. Keep?
- tat / tad before a noun is joined when it reads as a compound (tatsiddhaṃ,
  taccūrṇaṃ, tatpakvaṃ) and split when it is a pronoun; the agents were not
  consistent, and the pairs below show where. Is there a rule to follow?
- where a lemma and its gloss have lost their hyphen in the source, the words are
  split and the hyphen is not restored. Should it be restored?
